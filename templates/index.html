Glossary Terms

Polyuria

Abnormally large or frequent urination. One of the earliest signs of diabetes caused by excess sugar pulling fluids through the kidneys.

Polydipsia

Excessive and persistent thirst. Occurs when the body loses too much fluid through frequent urination.

Sudden Weight Loss

Unexplained rapid reduction in body weight. Happens when the body cannot use glucose and begins breaking down fat and muscle instead.

Polyphagia

Extreme, persistent hunger. Even after eating, cells starved of glucose cause constant hunger signals.

Genital Thrush

A yeast infection (Candida) in the genital area. High blood sugar creates an environment that promotes yeast overgrowth.

Visual Blurring

Blurry vision caused by fluid changes in the eye lens due to elevated blood sugar levels.

Partial Paresis

Partial loss of movement or muscle weakness. In diabetes, it can result from nerve damage (neuropathy) caused by prolonged high blood sugar.

Muscle Stiffness

Tightness or reduced flexibility in muscles. Diabetic neuropathy and poor circulation can contribute to this condition.

Alopecia

Hair loss or thinning. Diabetes-related hormonal imbalances and poor circulation can affect hair follicles.

Delayed Healing

Wounds or sores that heal slowly. High blood sugar impairs circulation and immune response making recovery slower.

Irritability

Mood changes and emotional instability. Blood sugar fluctuations directly affect brain chemistry leading to mood swings.

Obesity

Excessive body fat accumulation. A major risk factor for Type 2 diabetes as excess fat causes insulin resistance.
